\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the replication supports}

The main result supports a narrow empirical claim: under the tested harmful-request prompt and \noco{} condition, LLaMA 3.1 8B exhibits a tier-conditioned classifier compliance gap consistent with the earlier observation. The result survives a within-experiment multiplicity correction on the aggregate counts. Its disappearance under scratchpad prompting likewise reproduces the earlier qualitative pattern.

The cross-family evaluation limits the generalization of this result. Other evaluated models do not exhibit nominally significant harmful-request classifier gaps, and the persona preservation scenario does not supply a consistent transfer pattern.

\subsection{Scratchpad prompting changes the evaluation}

For LLaMA 3.1 8B, scratchpad prompting removes the measured tier gap. For Gemma 3, it had the effect of substantially increases harmful compliance in both tiers.

\subsection{Limitations and scope}
Some key limitations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Quantization and settings.} Quantization and other inference settings can affect behavior in unpredictable ways.
    \item \textbf{Preference conflicts.} The persona scenario may not be a good candidate to surface models' honesty behaviour as models are created in a way to be expected to preserve new characters they're assigned.
    \item \textbf{Measurement uncertainty.} The response judge comes from an evaluated model family, so shared-model effects are possible.
\end{itemize}
