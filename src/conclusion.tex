\section{Conclusion}

This study combines a brief mapping of AI safety research with a predominantly experimental evaluation of alignment faking in small language models. It reproduces an 11-point harmful-request compliance gap in LLaMA 3.1 8B without scratchpad prompting, and the measured gap disappears with scratchpad prompting. The remaining harmful-request configurations do not extend this signal, and the persona preservation scenario does not show a consistent transfer pattern.

The findings support a context-specific replication rather than a general claim that small models fake alignment or are incapable of doing so. They also expose important differences between substantive compliance, self-reported acceptance, and tier-aware scratchpad text. Future evaluations should establish baseline preferences, audit paired response records, strengthen human validation, and test sensitivity to checkpoints, prompt variants, and independent runs.
