The worry that machines might pursue goals different from their designers' intent has been the cornerstone of the field's early work, but it remained largely philosophical up until 2008, when \citet{omohundro2008basic} argued that goal-directed systems tend to develop sub-goals like self-preservation and resource acquisition, whatever their final goal may be, and the field started seeking true engineering solutions.

The alignment problem was more precisely defined by \citet{hubinger2019risks} and divided into outer and inner alignment; it shows that even if the training environment has correct specifications, the model may still end up with wrong objectives or mesa-objectives. This is analogous to animals in nature not presenting the explicit goal of reproducing or spreading their genes, but having instead pseudo-goals like finding food, shelter, or safety. This was later demonstrated as goal misgeneralization in deep reinforcement learning by \citet{langosco2022goal} and replicated with the transformer architecture in \citet{shah2022goal}.

Instrumental convergence behaviour has already been observed in LLMs by Anthropic: \citet{greenblatt_alignment_faking} found that a large model that thought its answers to some users would be used for retraining complied with harmful requests more often for those users, and its scratchpad reasoning often cited the wish to preserve its existing preferences. \citet{koorndijk_empirical_2025} adapted this setup to a small model, LLaMA 3 8B Instruct, and reported a similar prompt-induced compliance gap that scratchpad prompting reduced. Whether the result holds beyond that single small model is the question we test.
